# First layer not sticking

> Source: https://help.prusa3d.com/article/first-layer-issues_1804 · Retrieved 2026-11-11

The first layer is the base of the whole print. Problems here are the most common 3D printing issue, and a bad first layer makes every other kind of failure more likely.

## Signs

The first lines don't stick to the bed, curl up, get dragged around by the nozzle, or look patchy, too thin or too squashed.

## Common causes

- Dirty or greasy print surface (fingerprints are enough).
- Nozzle too far from or too close to the bed.
- Printing too fast, or the wrong nozzle or bed temperature.
- A full or partial clog in the nozzle.
- An uneven, bent or badly seated print sheet, or debris under it.

## How to fix it

1. Clean the print sheet with 90% or stronger isopropyl alcohol (IPA) and a paper towel. If IPA leaves residue, wash it with dish soap and warm (not hot) water. Don't touch the surface with your fingers afterwards.
2. Check the sheet sits flat on the bed with nothing underneath it.
3. Re-run the first layer calibration and adjust the nozzle height (on Prusa printers: Live Adjust Z, via LCD Menu, Calibration, First layer calibration). Don't lower the nozzle too far to force adhesion, because that can damage the sheet.
4. Slow down the first layers. Turn the speed knob counterclockwise to about 75% for the first three layers, then return to normal.
5. Use the slicer's recommended temperatures. For a material that won't stick, raise the bed temperature by 5–10 °C.
6. For PETG, ABS, ASA, nylon, PC and flexible filaments, use a thin layer of PVA glue stick. Never use superglue.
7. Turn on a brim in the slicer to give small parts more grip.
8. If lines are thin or missing, the nozzle may be clogged. See clogged-nozzle.md.
