# Clogged nozzle

> Sources: https://help.prusa3d.com/article/clogged-nozzle-core-one-core-one-l-xl-mk4-s-mk3-9-s_411823, https://help.prusa3d.com/article/cold-pull-26702-mk4s-13702-mk4-28702-mk3-9s-21702-mk3-9-23702-mk3-5-28702-mk3-5s-17702-xl_445071 · Retrieved 2026-11-11

## Signs

- Little or no filament comes out of the nozzle.
- Gaps or missing layers in the print.
- Filament curls up and sticks to the nozzle.
- A loud clicking from the extruder (the gears slipping) points to a clogged nozzle. A light clicking points to the gears.

## How to fix it

Heat the nozzle only from the printer's own menu, never with an outside heat source, and don't touch the hotend while it is hot.

1. Try unloading the filament completely. If it won't come out, the problem is more serious.
2. Clean the extruder gears. Brush debris out of the gear teeth with compressed air, a brass brush or a toothpick. {staff_only: true}
3. Push fresh filament through: open the extruder idler, heat the nozzle to 260 °C for PLA (280 °C if PETG or ASA was used), wait 2 minutes, then push about 40 cm of PLA in by hand. If plastic flows out again, the clog is cleared. If not, the nozzle probably needs replacing. {staff_only: true}
4. For a partial clog, run a cold pull (on Prusa printers: LCD Menu, Control, Cold Pull). Use at least 30 cm of PLA. The printer cools the nozzle, warms it slightly and pulls the filament out. Pull it upward by hand. A clean pull shows the shape of the nozzle with no dark specks or old colors. Repeat until it comes out clean. {staff_only: true}
5. If a bit of filament is visible at the top of the nozzle, heat to 280 °C and slowly pull it out with needle-nose pliers, then do a cold pull. {staff_only: true}

## Preventing clogs

- Store filament dry, ideally in a dry box with desiccant.
- For PLA in an enclosure, open the door or top vents so the hotend doesn't overheat (heat creep).
